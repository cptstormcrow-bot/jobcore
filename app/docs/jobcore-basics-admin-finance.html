Chapter 3 — Admin & Finance

The admin-finance module (modules/admin-finance/) is registered with requiredRoles: ['owner', 'admin'] — only the Admin/Owner roles see its nav entry, per modules/admin-finance/src/index.ts. It carries two personas in this manual: Admin, who runs the organization day to day, and Accountant (Finance), who reviews closed-out money. Both personas share the same module and the same registered screen; the module's source tree also contains a much larger set of lead, assignment, payout, and reporting screens that are not yet part of the registered route — called out honestly below rather than presented as reachable today.

What Is Actually Wired

adminFinanceModule.routes registers exactly one route, admin-finance, pointed at AdminFinanceRoot (modules/admin-finance/src/screens/AdminFinanceRoot.tsx). Its single navEntries item, "Admin & Finance", is what an Admin/Owner sees in the app's nav. AdminFinanceRoot is a small internal tab-switcher — Overview and Jobs — reading jobs for the signed-in org through an injected JobRepository port; it is explicitly documented in its own file header as read-only: "this screen never writes a job's stage. It has no access to any write path for that field at all." 🟢 Built & Live

The module also declares two jobActions: view-price-sheet ("View Price Sheet") and add-pay-period-note ("Add Pay Period Note"). These are registered on the module object and available to the host's job-action menu wiring, but this slice did not trace their end-to-end invocation path from a job card into a working screen, so they're marked 🟠 Built, Unverified rather than Built & Live.

Everything else under modules/admin-finance/src/screens/ — AssignmentBoardScreen, CloseOutQueueScreen, CloseOutReviewScreen, PayoutBatchScreen, PriceSheetListScreen, PriceSheetEditorScreen, ReimbursementQueueScreen, ReportsScreen, and AuditLogScreen — is real, substantial code (assign/close-out flows, payout batches, price sheets, reimbursements, reporting, an audit log) sitting behind a rich domain and money layer (src/domain/, src/money/, src/reporting/, src/flows/). None of it appears in adminFinanceModule.routes or navEntries, so none of it is reachable by navigating the running app today. 🟡 Built, Not Wired LeadInboxScreen (titled "Leads") and QualifyScreen are the exception — reachable via the Leads tab, described below. The walkthroughs below narrate this fuller screen set as the module's intended day-to-day shape, with every screen name honestly badged for its current reachability.

Admin's Typical Day

The Admin persona opens the app to the Admin & Finance nav entry and lands on AdminFinanceRoot's Overview tab 🟢 Built & Live, a one-line job count for the organization. Switching to the Jobs tab lists every job on file with its scheduled date, but with no filter, search, or action beyond viewing the list.

  1. Set up payment providers in advance. Before any job can reach state-completed, an Admin flips on the org's accepted payment methods in the payment_providers table (supabase/0010_payment_review.sql) — per-organization enabled flags plus client-safe config (a handle, a publishable key, never a secret). RLS on this table is admin-only (payment_providers_admin_all: organization_id = current_org_id() AND is_org_admin()), so this is exclusively an Admin setup task, done ahead of time rather than in the moment a job needs it. There is no dedicated in-app screen for this in the traced source — it is a direct data-setup task today. ⚪ Open Gap (Roadmap) for a proper Settings UI; the underlying table and RLS policy are 🟢 Built & Live.
  2. Triage new leads. The Leads tab lists unconverted leads newest first behind a New Lead button; opening one goes to LeadEditScreen (every field editable, Save); its Create Job button runs flows/qualify-lead.ts (QualifyScreen) to reach state-qualified. 🟢 Built & Live
  3. Assign the job. AssignmentBoardScreen, backed by flows/assign-job.ts and domain/assignment-warnings.ts, would drive state-scheduled → state-assigned, cross- checking the AxisPicker/TechColumn components and surfacing conflicts via WarningSheet. 🟡 Built, Not Wired
  4. Resolve a reschedule. When a tech or the state machine reports state-needs_reschedule, an Admin is the role positioned to re-run assignment and force the job back onto the board. The traced source has no dedicated reschedule-resolution screen; this is currently folded into the general assignment flow rather than a distinct UI. ⚪ Open Gap (Roadmap)
  5. Watch jobs across the board. Because Admin holds the broadest role, an Admin's Jobs-tab view (today, in AdminFinanceRoot) and the intended AssignmentBoardScreen are where every state in the lifecycle — from state-new through state-closed, including state-declined and state-cancelled branches — is visible in one place, even though only the read-only Jobs list is reachable today.
  6. Close out and pay. CloseOutQueueScreen and CloseOutReviewScreen (backed by flows/close-out-job.ts and domain/closeout.ts) would be where an Admin reviews jobs sitting at state-completed / state-paid and pushes them to state-closed — the same plain OFFICE transition Chapter 2 documents, with no precondition on that specific step. 🟡 Built, Not Wired
  7. Maintain price sheets. PriceSheetListScreen / PriceSheetEditorScreen, over data/price-sheet-repo.ts and data/price-sheet-feed.ts, are the intended home for the view-price-sheet jobAction's underlying data. 🟡 Built, Not Wired
  8. Check the audit trail. AuditLogScreen, over fixtures/audit-log.ts, is the intended record of who changed what — useful for confirming who forced a transition like a reschedule resolution or a manual close-out. 🟡 Built, Not Wired

Accountant's Typical Day

The Finance/Accountant persona shares the Admin role's app access but works the money layer rather than the operational one. Their day centers on jobs that have already reached state-completed or state-paid.

  1. Review reimbursements. ReimbursementQueueScreen, over data/reimbursement-repo.ts, is where tech-submitted expense reimbursements would be reviewed and approved. 🟡 Built, Not Wired
  2. Run payout batches. PayoutBatchScreen, over data/payout-batch-repo.ts and the money/ domain (billable-hours.ts, bonus.ts, commission.ts, payout-batch.ts, publish-plan.ts, rollup.ts), is where pay periods roll up billable hours, bonuses, and commission into a batch a tech gets paid from. The money-layer test coverage (money/__assertions__/: payout-freeze, publish-plan, rollup) suggests the calculation logic itself is more mature than its screen's reachability. 🟡 Built, Not Wired
  3. Confirm cash/check/P2P payments an Admin or tech reported. Jobs reaching state-paid via a non-webhook method (cash, check, or a P2P link) settle through a tech/office manual-confirm path governed by the payments_tech_confirm_whitelist trigger — an Accountant's review role here is to spot- check those manual confirmations against the job record, not to re-run the confirmation itself.
  4. Pull reports. ReportsScreen, over reporting/revenue.ts, reporting/group-by.ts, and reporting/slot-hit-rate.ts, is the intended revenue/utilization reporting surface. 🟡 Built, Not Wired
  5. Add a pay-period note. The add-pay-period-note jobAction is the per-job hook into this reconciliation work, though — as with view-price-sheet — this slice did not verify its end-to-end wiring from a job card. 🟠 Built, Unverified

Honesty Summary

Today, the Admin & Finance module gives an Admin or Accountant exactly one reachable screen — a read-only Overview/Jobs view — plus two unverified jobActions and a fully admin-gated payment_providers setup table. The lead-to-close-out-to-payout pipeline described above is real, tested code sitting one routing step away from being reachable: registering the remaining screens in adminFinanceModule.routes/navEntries (or in per-screen sub- navigation inside AdminFinanceRoot) is the open gap between "built" and "usable."
