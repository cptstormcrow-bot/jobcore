Chapter 4 — Field Tech

The Field Tech module (modules/field-tech) is where the job spine actually gets driven: a tech's day physically walks assigned → accepted → en_route → in_progress → completed → paid. It registers with requiredRoles: ['owner', 'admin', 'tech'] and navEntries: [{ icon: 'construct', label: 'Field Tech', order: 20 }] (modules/field-tech/src/index.ts). Every screen in this module is ZERO financial fields by construction — hours, commissions, reimbursements, and bonus live only on the Earnings stop below, never on the job-detail or completion screens. 🟢 Built & Live

A Typical Day — Service Technician

Marcus is a field tech for a two-truck outfit. This is the fullest persona walkthrough in the manual because his day is the one that actually moves a job's status, stop by stop, through transitionJob calls that this module's own domain/transition.ts is the sole call site for (a field-tech ESLint override forbids importing applyTransition directly from @jobcore/core anywhere else in this module).

Morning: Day List

Marcus opens the app and lands on DayList (screens/DayList.tsx) 🟢 Built & Live, the module's default route when no per-job flow is active (FieldTechRoot's subView === 'jobs' tab). The list shows only jobs assigned to Marcus — not by any client-side filter, but purely because TechJobRepository.listAssignedToTech is scoped server-side by RLS on the jobs/job_assignments tables. Each row is color-coded via the shared JOB_STATUS_COLORS map, so a newly assigned job stands out from one already accepted or en_route.

First Stop: Greeting

Tapping a freshly assigned job opens Greeting (screens/Greeting.tsx) 🟢 Built & Live, offering Marcus accept/decline on a job in state-assigned. Decline requires a non-empty reason — the screen writes a job_notes row (kind='decline_reason') before calling transitionJob(job, 'declined', ctx), routing the job back to the office (see Chapter 2's declined branch). Accept is deliberately passive: silence leaves status untouched, so Greeting just dismisses and lets Marcus proceed — there is no explicit "accept" transition call here, because state-accepted is reached by the tech simply moving forward, not by a dedicated button firing its own transition.

Heading Out: On My Way

From StartChecklist (screens/StartChecklist.tsx) 🟢 Built & Live Marcus taps "On my way," which fires transitionJob(job, 'en_route', ctx) — this is the same jobActions entry (id: 'on-my-way') the shared Job Detail action bar exposes, registered in fieldTechModule.jobActions. The job is now state-en_route. Chapter 2's cancellation-window rule applies exactly here: en_route is the last state from which the office can still cancel the job out from under Marcus — once he taps Start, that window is closed.

On Site: Start Checklist

Still on StartChecklist, Marcus works through the start checklist and captures before-photos, each recorded through the offline mutation primitives in @jobcore/core's offline/queue (makeIdempotencyKey, queued mutations) so a dead-zone driveway doesn't lose the record. Submitting the checklist calls ChecklistRepository.submitInstance, then records the submission in the module's sync-readable singleton (so ctx.hasSubmittedStartChecklist reads synchronously true), then calls transitionJob(job, 'in_progress', ctx) — the state-completed precondition Chapter 2 calls the start-checklist precondition. Job is now state-in_progress. 🟢 Built & Live

Mid-Job: Field Job Detail

Marcus can check job specifics any time on FieldJobDetail (screens/FieldJobDetail.tsx) 🟢 Built & Live, a module-local detail screen — deliberately not the shared apps/mobile JobDetail.tsx, since field-tech's isolation rule forbids importing anything from apps/mobile. Tap-to-call uses a caller-supplied customerPhone prop; ZERO financial fields render here, by the same construction rule as every other screen in this module.

Finishing Up: Completion

When the work is done, Marcus opens Completion (screens/Completion.tsx) 🟢 Built & Live. He checks off the completion checklist, captures at least one after-photo (ports.photos.create, kind 'after'), and types quality notes — the submit button stays disabled until all three are satisfied. Ordering here is load-bearing: the quality-notes job_notes row is written and the in-memory sync probe updated before transitionJob(job, 'completed', ctx) runs, so ctx.hasAfterPhotos/ctx.hasQualityNotes read synchronously true when the core's completed precondition (packages/core/src/state-machine.ts) evaluates them. If Marcus's job has a non-customer payer (a partner-billed job), ctx.hasPaymentRecord is satisfied automatically — field-tech never records a payment itself, by design. The job is now state-completed.

Collecting Payment from the Homeowner

For a customer-paid job, completed does not by itself reach paid — the requirePaymentSettled precondition in packages/core/src/state-machine.ts gates that transition. Here is the full sequence, honest about what's built, what's unverified, and what's still a gap.

Handing Off to the Homeowner

Once Marcus submits Completion, the customer is handed off via a signed, expiring, single-job-scoped QR token (issue_handoff_token RPC, modules/payment-review/src/domain/handoff.ts) to the public handoff web app at web/handoff/. 🟠 Built, Unverified QR code rendering is explicitly out of scope here — only the token/URL is issued today; Marcus hands the phone to the homeowner or the homeowner scans a code Marcus doesn't yet have a screen to display.

The Missing Satisfaction Gate

Storyboarded, not built — a homeowner-satisfaction confirmation step ahead of payment capture, the moment where the homeowner would sign off on the work before being asked to pay. No such gate exists in state-machine.ts, the payment-review module, or the handoff screens. ⚪ Open Gap (Roadmap) Today the homeowner goes straight from Marcus's handoff into picking a payment method.

The Four Payment Methods

On the handoff site, the homeowner picks from whichever methods the organization has enabled (Chapter 3's Admin persona flips these switches in advance, before any job reaches state-completed):

  • Card (Stripe) — supabase/functions/stripe-webhook/index.ts confirms the matching payments row via signature-verified webhook. No card data is ever read or logged. 🟠 Built, Unverified
  • Card (PayPal) — supabase/functions/paypal-webhook/index.ts confirms via PayPal's server-side signature-verification API. 🟠 Built, Unverified
  • Cash — modules/payment-review/src/domain/providers/cash.ts, not webhook-capable by design; Marcus (or the office) confirms receipt in person, which sets status='manual_confirmed' under a whitelist trigger that restricts a non-admin caller to only that status/confirmed-at/confirmed-by. 🟠 Built, Unverified
  • Check — modules/payment-review/src/domain/providers/check.ts, same manual-confirmation pattern as Cash. 🟠 Built, Unverified
  • P2P link (Venmo / CashApp / Zelle) — modules/payment-review/src/domain/providers/p2p-link.ts, an org-configured handle/URL the homeowner pays to directly, settled by the same manual confirmation as Cash/Check. 🟠 Built, Unverified

Once a payments row reaches confirmed (webhook) or manual_confirmed (manual entry), the job settles into state-paid.

The Mark Paid Gap Marcus Never Sees

There's a screen for manually marking a job paid — a jobAction (id mark-paid) on paymentReviewModule (modules/payment-review/src/index.ts) — but it is restricted to requiredRoles: ['owner', 'admin'] (OFFICE), not tech, so Marcus would never see it even if it were reachable. It isn't: confirmed by direct search, the payment-review module is NOT registered in apps/mobile/src/modules.ts today — no reference to it exists there. The module, its screen, and this jobAction are unreachable from the running mobile app. 🔴 Not Reachable See Chapter 6 for the module's full architecture/status detail.

Office Closes It Out

The final step, paid → closed, is a plain OFFICE-role transition with no precondition — it happens back at the office, after Marcus has already moved on to his next job. His own visible arc on this job ends at completed; everything from the QR handoff onward runs outside his screens.

End of Day: Earnings

Marcus checks Earnings (screens/Earnings.tsx) 🟢 Built & Live — the one screen in this module that's allowed to show money. Derived hours use job.inProgressAt/job.completedAt, the device-clock occurred_at stamps written by applyTransition — never synced_at, which can lag behind under the offline queue and would silently shift a tech's hours if used for payroll math. Commissions, reimbursements, and bonus render alongside (bonus always shows a real dollar figure, even $0.00, via a null-object NullBonusRule when no rule applies). If Marcus disagrees with a figure, he can file a dispute — a note-only job_notes row (kind='dispute') that never calls transitionJob or canTransitionJob; filing a dispute cannot change job status.

The 8-Hour-Day Efficiency Note

Every screen above deliberately does one thing: DayList lists, Greeting accepts/declines, StartChecklist starts, FieldJobDetail informs, Completion completes, Earnings reports. None of them cross into another's job — Completion never shows pay figures, Earnings never fires a transition, FieldJobDetail never renders a financial field. For a tech running four to six jobs in an eight-hour day, that narrowness is the point: each screen answers exactly the one question Marcus has open at that moment, with nothing else competing for his attention between doors.
